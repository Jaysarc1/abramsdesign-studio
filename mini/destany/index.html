Born in Sinaloa, raised on brass.

Banda sinaloense is the big brass sound of Sinaloa, on Mexico's Pacific coast: a dozen or more horns, clarinets and drums carrying the whole party, no guitars required.

1800s

Brass comes to town

Town and military brass bands spread through Sinaloa. Polkas, waltzes and marches, many arriving with European immigrants through the port of Mazatlán, mix with Mexican sones.

1938

La madre de todas las bandas

Cruz Lizárraga founds Banda El Recodo in El Recodo, Sinaloa. It's still touring today and is known as the mother of all bands.

1990s

SoCal catches the fever

Technobanda and the quebradita craze fill dance floors across Los Angeles and Orange County.

Today

Every party, every floor

Banda plays bodas, quinceañeras and Saturday-night bailes from Mazatlán to Buena Park, and the dance starts with one simple bounce.

Before you ask.

The questions Destany hears most, answered in one place.

Do you teach on weekends?

Right now classes run on Monday and Tuesday evenings. If a weekend class would work better for you, let Destany know.

Can you offer later classes?

Monday classes start at 6:30 PM and Tuesday classes at 7:45 PM. Tell Destany what time works for you.

If I bring a friend, do they pay full price?

Friend pricing is Destany's call. Ask her before class and she'll tell you what's on.

I've never danced before. Is that okay?

Yes. Every class on the schedule is beginner friendly, no experience needed. You can practice the basic step right here first.
